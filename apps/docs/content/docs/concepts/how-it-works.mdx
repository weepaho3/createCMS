---
title: How createCMS works
description: The data model and its database tables, explained on one machine, a multiplane animation camera, one scene at a time.
---

createCMS stores content the way an animation studio shoots a film on a multiplane camera. An entry is a film. Its blocks are cels, transparent sheets with a drawing on each, stacked on the camera's glass planes. A commit is one exposure: the camera photographs the stack, and the negative records exactly which cels were in it. A branch is a pointer clip at the end of a strip of exposed frames, and publishing threads a strip into the projector.

This page walks through that one machine, part by part. Each scene zooms into one station, shows what moves, and names the database tables behind it, so by the end you can read the schema the way you read the camera.

The scenes are interactive. Hover a part, or focus a figure and use the arrow keys.

## The camera

Here is the whole machine. A column on a heavy foot carries three glass planes and, at the top, the camera pointing straight down at them. The cabinet of spare cels stands to the left. At the front, a developed film strip lies on a light table. The projector stands on its pedestal to the right. Hover a station, or press 1 to 5.

The stations map onto the model like this:

| Station             | In the model                                        | Tables                        |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| 01 The cel cabinet  | Every block version ever written                    | `block_versions`              |
| 02 The glass planes | One entry's block tree, a cel per block             | `roots`, `block_versions`     |
| 03 The camera       | A commit: one exposure and its negative             | `commits`, `commit_snapshots` |
| 04 The film strip   | The chain of commits, with a pointer clip per branch | `commits`, `branches`         |
| 05 The projector    | A published branch, always showing its last frame   | `publications`                |

The following scenes take the stations in the order content flows through them: cels on the planes, an exposure, the strip, the projector.

## Scene 1: the planes

An entry is one film. In the database it is a row in `roots`: an id, the collection it belongs to, and its live slug. Everything the entry contains lies on the planes as cels.

The content is a tree of blocks, and the planes are what make the tree visible. A block is a cel. A block that has children is a plane: its children lie on it, side by side, in `children` order. In the figure the hero cel fills the bottom plane, the text cel lies on the middle plane, and the top plane carries the gallery, whose three image cels lie on it in order. The entry's root block is the whole stack. Hover a cel, or walk through the blocks with the arrow keys.

  name="scene-1"

  height={620}
/>

Every cel, the hero, the text, the gallery and each image, is a row in `block_versions`. That row is the whole block: its `type`, its `properties` as JSON, the ordered `children` ids, and a `deleted` flag. The rows under the figure show the one you point at. Two things are worth noticing already:

- The tree is not stored as a nested document. Each block is its own row, and a parent only lists its children's ids. The gallery does not contain its images, it names them, and they lie on its plane. That is what lets a later exposure swap one cel without touching the plane or the cels next to it, which the next scenes build on.
- The root block is a block like any other, with the same kind of row. It carries the entry's own properties, including the draft slug (`__slug`), which only becomes the live `roots.slug` when the branch is published.
