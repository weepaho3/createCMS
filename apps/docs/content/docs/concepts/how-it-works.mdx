title: How createCMS works
description: The data model and its database tables, told as a bookbinder's shop, one scene at a time.
---

createCMS stores content the way a careful bookbinder's shop would run: every entry is a book, every block is a printed sheet, every commit is a table of contents, and a branch is nothing more than a bookmark. This page walks through that shop station by station. Each scene zooms into one part of the model and names the database tables behind it, so by the end you can read the schema the way you read the shop.

The scenes are interactive. Hover a part, or focus a figure and use the arrow keys.

## The shop

Here is the whole shop at a glance. The street is at the front: a shop window with the books that are live, and a door. Behind the counter lies the workshop that produces them. Hover a station, or press 1 to 7.

The stations map onto the model like this:

| Station                | In the model                                       | Tables                                    |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| 01 The books           | Entries of a collection                            | `roots`                                   |
| 02 The press           | A change prints one new block version              | `block_versions`                          |
| 03 The archive         | Every block version ever written                   | `block_versions`                          |
| 04 The card index      | Commits, their snapshots, and the branches on them | `commits`, `commit_snapshots`, `branches` |
| 05 The writing desk    | Merge requests and conflicts                       | `merge_requests`, `merge_conflicts`       |
| 06 The binding machine | Publishing a branch                                | `publications`                            |
| 07 The shop window     | What visitors read                                 | `getPublishedContent`                     |

The following scenes take the stations in the order content flows through them.

## Scene 1: the book

An entry is one book. In the database it is a row in `roots`: an id, the collection it belongs to, and its live slug. In the figure that row is the front cover with its name plate, `books` and `/handbook`.

The content of the book is a tree of blocks, and the tree is where the metaphor earns its keep. The book itself is the root block. Its children are the chapters, in order. A chapter is a nested block: its children are the pages under its register tab. A page is a leaf block, a sheet with a title and a body and no children of its own. Hover the cover, a tab or a page, or walk the stack with the arrow keys.

<Scene
  name="scene-1"
  title="Fig 1: one entry as an open book, chapters as register tabs, pages as sheets"
  height={600}
/>

Every one of these, the book, each chapter and each page, is a row in `block_versions`. That row is the whole block: its `type`, its `properties` as JSON, the ordered `children` ids, and a `deleted` flag. The rows under the figure show the one you point at. Two things are worth noticing already:

- The tree is not stored as a nested document. Each block is its own row, and a parent only lists its children's ids. A chapter does not contain its pages, it names them. That is what lets a later commit replace one page without touching the chapter or the other pages, which the next scenes build on.
- The root block is a block like any other, with the same kind of row. It carries the book's own properties, including the draft slug (`__slug`), which only becomes the live `roots.slug` when the branch is published.
